Where the pressure comes from. Ghana's price is fixed by COCOBOD at GHS 800 per bag, so there is no domestic spot market to sell into. The pressure is cross-border: Cote d'Ivoire farmgate ran near $1.33/kg against Ghana's $0.89 to $1.25/kg as the cedi fell about 27% in 2022, a gap that ran several times the 6.00% threshold. That is the season Ghana lost volume to smuggling.
Method: Python, deterministic pseudonymisation, panel reconstruction, incentive-threshold derivation. Names replaced with stable pseudonyms and character notes dropped before anything left the source, verified by an automated check. Strategic Default Pressure Index · counterparty default-risk on a real cocoa season
What it found

From booking losses to pricing the incentive that causes them.

When you fund a delivery before it exists, the counterparty decides whether to deliver on economics you can see in advance. The index makes that explicit: a supplier who takes the advance and sells elsewhere forfeits the buffer and commission withheld against the bag, GHS 48 on an GHS 800 bag, so default becomes rational the moment the market pays more than 6.00% over the advance. That threshold is derived from the contract, not chosen.

Across three dated reports the open gap was worked from 297 bags to 111, delivery recovery rising from 80.0% to 94.7%, and the season closed in net credit. The intra-season peak of 654 bags was closed by 83% while funding kept growing, which is the harder version of the problem.

Exposure was carried, not spread: 4 of 36 clerks held half the open gap and 9 held 80%. A uniform funding policy would have mispriced almost all of them, which is why the policy behind the book was tiered by clerk. The model, the code and a de-identified season are published in the open, so every figure here can be checked rather than taken on trust.
