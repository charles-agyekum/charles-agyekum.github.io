NHS Cancer Pathway — the 62-Day Standard.

Back to all work→
The question

Is this cohort meeting the standard — and is the aggregate hiding anyone?

The NHS 62-day standard says a cancer patient should start treatment within 62 days of urgent referral, and a provider is on target when 85% or more of pathways meet it. The data gives you dates; it does not give you the standard. The benchmark comes from policy — knowing where to find it, and that it was reformed in October 2023, is the analyst's job, not the dataset's.

Honesty note: the 500-pathway extract is training data; the standard it is tested against is the real one. The method — derive the measure, verify the cohort, benchmark against cited policy, then challenge the aggregate — is exactly the method for the real thing.

Step 1 · Scope check before any headline

A breach rate over an incomplete cohort is a wrong breach rate. First question to the data: does anyone have no treatment date?

SELECT
  COUNT(*)                              AS Total_Patients,
  COUNT(Treatment_Start_Date)           AS Treated,
  COUNT(*) - COUNT(Treatment_Start_Date) AS Untreated
FROM CancerPathway;
-- 0 untreated: all 500 pathways are in scope.
Step 2 · Derive the measure, band the cohort

The wait is computed from the dates — not taken from a pre-made column — and each pathway is banded against the 62-day line with CASE.

SELECT
  NHS_Number,
  DATEDIFF(day, Referral_Date, Treatment_Start_Date) AS Days_Ref_to_Treatment,
  CASE WHEN DATEDIFF(day, Referral_Date, Treatment_Start_Date) > 62
       THEN 'Breached' ELSE 'Within 62-day standard' END AS Status_62_Day
FROM CancerPathway;
Step 3 · The headline, against the cited standard
SELECT
  COUNT(*) AS Total_Patients,
  SUM(CASE WHEN DATEDIFF(day, Referral_Date, Treatment_Start_Date) > 62
      THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS Breached,
  CAST(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN DATEDIFF(day, Referral_Date, Treatment_Start_Date) > 62
      THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) / COUNT(*) AS DECIMAL(5,1)) AS Breach_Rate_Pct
FROM CancerPathway;
-- 99.2% within standard vs the 85% target — comfortably on target.

Step 4 · Challenge the aggregate

A 99.2% headline can be process excellence — or a strong majority overshadowing a weak provider. The same instinct that says a portfolio-level risk number can hide a concentrated counterparty says a trust-level compliance number can hide a struggling provider. So the aggregate gets challenged, not celebrated:

SELECT
  Treatment_Provider,
  COUNT(*) AS Patients,
  AVG(CAST(DATEDIFF(day, Referral_Date, Treatment_Start_Date) AS DECIMAL(5,1))) AS Avg_Wait_Days,
  SUM(CASE WHEN DATEDIFF(day, Referral_Date, Treatment_Start_Date) > 62
      THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS Breaches
FROM CancerPathway
GROUP BY Treatment_Provider
ORDER BY Breaches DESC, Avg_Wait_Days DESC;
The recommendation

Report compliance at provider level, not cohort level — the 62-day headline is a floor for scrutiny, not a ceiling. And extend the monitoring to the other two reformed standards — the 28-day Faster Diagnosis Standard (75%) and the 31-day Decision-to-Treat Standard (96%) — which need diagnosis and decision dates this extract does not carry; that is the next data request, named before it is needed.

Benchmark source — external policy, not derived from the dataset

NHS England Cancer Waiting Times Standards, reformed 1 October 2023: 28-day Faster Diagnosis 75% · 31-day Decision-to-Treat 96% · 62-day Referral-to-Treatment 85%. Verified 5 July 2026 against england.nhs.uk — changes to cancer waiting times standards.
